*Thesis:* Strategies fail on the one assumption nobody was paid to attack; red team coaching builds the adversarial thinking that finds it while it's still cheap to change.

**Post copy:**

```
Most strategies don't fail because the logic was bad.

They fail because of one assumption nobody in the room was paid to attack.

That's the entire premise of red team coaching.

Not consulting. Not a 60-page deck. Coaching your team to think like the competitor, the regulator, the market, and the disgruntled customer — before reality forces the lesson at full price.

The cheapest moment to find a fatal flaw is while the decision is still on the whiteboard. The most expensive moment is after the launch, the hire, or the acquisition.

A good red team turns that around. In a single session a team can:
→ Run a premortem — "it's 18 months out and this failed. Why?"
→ Surface the assumption everyone agreed with too fast
→ Pressure-test the plan against a motivated adversary
→ Leave with owned, dated actions — not just anxiety

The opportunity for businesses and organizations isn't "avoid risk." It's decision quality as a repeatable capability — installed in your team, not rented from a firm.

The teams that win aren't the ones with the boldest plans. They're the ones whose plans survived contact with someone whose job was to break them.

What's the last big decision your team made that nobody was asked to argue against?
```
